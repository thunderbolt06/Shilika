You get featured in CoinDesk, Cointelegraph, and Decrypt in 2026 by pitching a specific, verifiable story to the right beat reporter - not by sending a press release or buying a "guaranteed feature." The winning formula is: a dated news peg, original data or exclusive access, named on-the-record sources, and a short pitch tailored to each outlet's editorial style.

Build a story, not an announcement

Editors are looking for something their readers would care about: a category shift, surprising on-chain data, a regulatory development, a major upgrade with measurable impact, original research, or a credible contrarian take. A funding announcement is weak on its own; "our protocol processed $500M in volume with zero exploits in 18 months" gives a reporter a story.

Find the actual reporter

Read the last 12-20 bylines in your category at each outlet. Identify reporters covering DeFi, infrastructure, regulation, stablecoins, gaming, AI-crypto, APAC, or whatever matches your story, then reference their recent work and explain why your story fits their beat.

Write a pitch that gets opened

Keep it short, plain-text, and attachment-free. A practical structure:

Subject: The story in one clause, not a vague teaser.

First sentence: The news fact and date.

Second: Why it matters to that reporter's specific beat.

Third: What you are offering - exclusive data, interview, embargoed report, or founder access.

Close: Confirm you can provide assets and sources within 24 hours.

For CoinDesk, aim for under 180 words; for Decrypt, under 150 words. Avoid PDFs, videos, and generic "we're excited to announce" language.

Use embargoes strategically

Offer an embargo 7-10 days before launch, confirm it in writing with the exact publication time, and give the reporter enough runway to brief editors and prepare the piece. Schedule a 30-45 minute founder briefing within 72 hours of acceptance, and keep the founder reachable shortly before publication.
shilikajain

For a multi-outlet launch, coordinate timing carefully: let the flagship story land first or simultaneously, then release supporting material and partner announcements with a slight delay so the editorial coverage remains the primary news event.
