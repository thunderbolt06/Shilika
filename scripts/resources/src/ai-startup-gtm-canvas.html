The AI Startup GTM Canvas

One page that forces the ten go-to-market decisions most AI teams leave implicit, a 90-day worksheet that turns them into a plan, and an illustrative filled example so you can see the level of specificity that makes a canvas useful.

How to fill it in

  1. Fill the canvas in pencil, in one 90-minute session with the founders. Leave a box blank rather than writing something vague.
  2. Every box should contain something a skeptical investor could check: a named segment, a price, a number, a competitor.
  3. Write the riskiest assumption in Risks, then design the first 30 days of the plan to test it.
  4. Revisit monthly. A canvas that never changes is a canvas nobody reads.

1 · MarketWHERE
Which segment first, and why now? What changed (model capability, cost, regulation, platform shift) that makes this possible this year?
2 · ICPWHO
Company type and size, trigger event, buyer title, user title. Who signs, who uses, who can block?
3 · Problem
The painful job in the buyer's words. What does it cost them today, in hours, money or risk?
4 · Alternatives
What they use now: incumbent, in-house build, general-purpose LLM, agency, nothing.
5 · Positioning
For [ICP] who [trigger/problem], [product] is a [category] that [key value]. Unlike [alternative], we [unique capability + proof].
6 · Pricing
Model (seat, usage, outcome, platform fee), entry price, expansion path. Does gross margin survive inference costs at scale?
7 · Motion
Product-led, sales-led, partner-led or community-led? Sales cycle length? Who closes the first 10 deals?
8 · ChannelsMAX 3
The three channels you will test first, each with a budget and an owner. Why will your ICP see you there?
9 · Metrics
One north-star metric, plus leading indicators: activation rate, qualified pipeline, CAC, payback, net revenue retention.
10 · RisksTEST FIRST
Riskiest assumptions: model commoditisation, data access, trust and security review, platform dependency, a big player shipping the same feature.

Illustrative example · fictional company

Worked example: "Ledgerlight"

A made-up AI startup used only to show the level of detail. Not a real company, client or result.

1 · Market

Mid-market finance teams in the US and UK. LLMs can now read messy invoices and contracts reliably enough for a human-in-the-loop review flow; this was not practical two years ago.

2 · ICP

B2B SaaS companies with 200 to 1,000 staff, 3 to 10 people in finance, just switched ERP or failed an audit. Buyer: VP Finance. User: AP lead. Blocker: IT security.

3 · Problem

"We spend the first week of every month chasing invoice mismatches." Month-end close slips by days; errors surface in the audit.

4 · Alternatives

Manual review in spreadsheets, the AP module of their ERP, an outsourced bookkeeping firm.

5 · Positioning

For finance teams who close the month late because of invoice mismatches, Ledgerlight is an AI accounts-payable reviewer that flags errors before they post. Unlike ERP rules, it reads the contract, not just the PO.

6 · Pricing

Platform fee plus per-invoice usage. Annual contract, entry tier for under 2,000 invoices a month. Margin checked at 3x current volume.

7 · Motion

Sales-led with a free 14-day pilot on last month's invoices. Founder closes first 10 deals. Target cycle: 30 to 45 days.

8 · Channels

1. Founder LinkedIn to VP Finance audience. 2. ERP marketplace listing plus integration. 3. Earned media and podcasts in finance-ops trade press.

9 · Metrics

North star: invoices reviewed per week. Leading: pilots started, pilot-to-paid rate, security review pass rate, CAC payback under 12 months.

10 · Risks

IT security blocks pilots. ERP vendor ships a similar feature. Test first: can we pass a security questionnaire in under a week? (Prepare a trust page and SOC 2 roadmap.)
