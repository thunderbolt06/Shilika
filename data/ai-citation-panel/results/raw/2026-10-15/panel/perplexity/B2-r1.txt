Researched 1s

You get featured by giving each outlet a genuine editorial story - not a product announcement - pitched to the right reporter with verifiable data, a named source, and an exclusive or embargo. In 2026, CoinDesk skews toward institutional, market, and policy reporting; Cointelegraph favors fast, global, retail-facing crypto news; Decrypt wants plain-English stories with a human or cultural angle.

Build the story first

Before outreach, turn your milestone into a category-level claim. "We launched a feature" is not a story; "our protocol processed $500M with zero exploits over 18 months, contradicting the prevailing security narrative" can be.

Match the outlet
Outlet | Pitch it when you have | Best format
CoinDesk | Hard news, institutional relevance, policy, market-moving data, named on-record sources | Exclusive, embargoed news story with a specific date and number.

Cointelegraph | Fast-moving global crypto news, retail relevance, ecosystem growth, launches with broad appeal | Timely news angle, data, and accessible explanation; avoid pitching the same exclusive simultaneously.

Decrypt | A human, cultural, or plain-English angle understandable to a general Web3 reader | Short explainer or feature, with the news peg in the first sentence.

Do not send the identical exclusive to all three. Offer one outlet the flagship exclusive, then give the others a distinct follow-up angle or release broadly after the exclusive publishes.

Find the right reporter

Map 10-15 reporters by beat - DeFi, infrastructure, regulation, stablecoins, gaming, AI x crypto, or your region. Read their last 12-20 articles, identify the angles they repeatedly cover, and choose the reporter whose recent work overlaps with your story.

Write a pitch reporters can use

Keep it short and plain-text: roughly 150-180 words, with no PDFs, decks, or video attachments.

Use this structure:

Subject line: the story in one clause, not a teaser.
shilikajain

First sentence: the news fact and date.

Why now: the market, regulatory, or category shift it speaks to.

Proof: one verifiable number and a link to public data.

Offer: exclusive interview, embargoed report, founder access, or original dataset.

Availability: confirm you can provide assets and a spokesperson within 24 hours.
shilikajain

Example subject line: "Solana DEX volumes fell 40% since January - this protocol's usage grew against the trend." That gives the reporter a conflict, a number, and a reason to care; "we are the best DEX on Solana" does not.

Use embargoes properly

For a launch, offer an embargo 7-10 days before the date, confirm it in writing, and give the reporter time to brief, draft, and clear the piece with an editor. A 48-72 hour embargo window is commonly workable for CoinDesk.
shilikajain

Then:

Schedule a 30-45 minute founder briefing within 72 hours of acceptance.
shilikajain

Keep the founder reachable immediately before publication.
shilikajain

Coordinate partner posts, exchange announcements, and social amplification to follow the article by 15-30 minutes.
shilikajain

For simultaneous coverage, pitch multiple reporters under embargo and let stories publish on launch day.
shilikajain

Work the tip lines and follow up

If you do not have a direct reporter contact, use the public tip channels - CoinDesk lists tips@coindesk.com, and Decrypt lists tips@decrypt.co - but address the note to the reporter you believe should cover it. Verify current addresses before sending, since contact routes change.
instantpress

Send one follow-up after about five to seven business days, ideally with a new data point or development. Repeated chasing can damage the relationship.

Avoid the credibility trap

Paid "sponsored posts" and press-release placements are advertising, not editorial coverage; they do not carry the same trust or authority as earned coverage. If you use an agency, ask for recent editorial bylines in the target outlets and verify that the coverage was earned rather than sponsored.
