# Competitor analysis for AI-engine visibility (audit date 2026-10-15, revised 2026-10-16)

Scope: High Vibe PR, EAK Digital / EAK Wire, Coinbound, Outset PR, Wachsman, MarketAcross in depth; Guava Studio, Serotonin, Crackle PR, Aiken House, 5W, GrackerAI as short notes.
Health score for this area (how well Shilika is placed against these competitors for AI-engine visibility): **5 / 10** (reasoning in section 10).

## 0. Headline findings (read this first)

1. **Naming and citing are two different games.** MarketAcross is the most-named competitor in AI answers (18 of 92 usable cells, average list position 2.8) but its own site is cited in only 2 cells. Coinbound is named in 8 cells yet its own domain is cited in 15, the most of any agency and second only to shilikajain.com (37) across the whole panel. The first wins on third-party description (Clutch, marketplaces, roundups, press releases), the second on owning the pages the engines retrieve.
2. **The earlier draft of this report under-counted four competitors because it only used the 25 cells for the "A" prompts.** The panel has since grown to 92 usable first-run cells (A to E prompt sets). With the full set, High Vibe PR (6 cells), EAK (8), Outset PR (10) and Wachsman (7) are all named by AI engines. Corrected in section 2 and section 11.
3. **Shilika owns the niche and the regional prompts, not the head terms.** She is in the body of ChatGPT 5 of 5 and Gemini 4 of 5 answers to the five regional "E" prompts (Korea, Japan, APAC, Dubai, India), and 1 of 5 in each of ChatGPT, Claude, Perplexity and AI Mode on the fractional (A) prompts (Gemini 0 of 5). She is NOT named in the ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini answers to the head-term list prompts B1 (best Web3 PR agencies), C1 (best PR firms for AI startups) or D1 (best PR firms for cybersecurity startups). Those lists are filled by MarketAcross, Wachsman, Coinbound, Outset, EAK (Web3), High Vibe, Crackle, 5W, Treble (AI) and Eskenzi, Highwire, Merritt, Inkhouse (cyber).
4. **The citation layer is mostly agency-authored or syndicated listicles.** Across 92 cells the most-cited non-Shilika domains are coinbound.io (15), everything-pr.com (10), marketerhire.com (10), techbullion.com (10), chainpeak.io (9), mexc.com (9), crypticweb3.com (8), cracklepr.com (8), icoda.io (7), luvkaizen.com (7). Claude itself says in B1 that "most 2026 rankings are written or sponsored by the agencies themselves" and that MEXC "news" roundups putting Outset PR first "read like paid placements", and then still repeats the Outset award claim.
5. **llms.txt is hygiene, not the differentiator.** Of the six, only Coinbound (own domain) and EAK Wire have a working llms.txt. MarketAcross (most named), Outset (second most named) and Wachsman have none.
6. **The six do not compete in cybersecurity AI answers at all.** D-prompt answers (cyber PR) name none of the six; the competing set there is a different one (Eskenzi PR, Highwire, Merritt Group, Inkhouse, 10Fold, Code Red and others).

- **Third-party presence.** DesignRush profile (https://www.designrush.com/agency/profile/high-vibe-pr); The PR Net "Next Gen Awards 2026" honoree (announced through the same syndicated release network, a weak independent signal); named in ICODA roundups (icoda.io/ai/best-ai-driven-pr-agencies, icoda.io/ai/best-geo-agencies-for-crypto), a HackerNoon "best PR agencies who help brands show up in generative search" piece, and a Nerdbot Web3 roundup. (3) A small but consistent third-party footprint (DesignRush, ICODA roundups, HackerNoon, award release) that echoes the claim.

## 6. What the pattern says

1. **Named is not cited.** MarketAcross, Outset, Wachsman and Serotonin are named without their own domains being cited (2, 1, 1 and 0 citations). Coinbound, Crackle and High Vibe are cited because engines retrieve their own pages. Two different routes to visibility, and the third-party route is cheaper to start but depends on other people's pages.
2. **Self-ranked and sponsored listicles feed the answers, and the engines know it.** The engines' own caveats (Claude B1; the WebSearch summaries) say most lists are agency-authored. They are retrieved anyway. Credibility and date-stamp risk are real: Coinbound's page shows both June and April 2026 stamps and a 2024 heading.
3. **Prices and reviews are quoted.** Claude's cost answers reproduce Coinbound's ranges and quote MarketAcross prices via third-party chips; MarketAcross and Coinbound have 5.0 Clutch profiles with 8 to 9 reviews. Whoever publishes the comparison and price layer shapes the answer.
4. **llms.txt does not separate winners.** Of the four brands named most (MarketAcross 18, Outset 10, Coinbound 8, EAK 8), only Coinbound and EAK (Wire) have one. Low confidence (92 cells, one run each).
5. **Regional and AI-startup prompts reward focus.** MarketAcross and Outset take the regional E prompts through earned-media and roundups; Crackle and High Vibe take the AI C prompts through category-specific pages. Shilika wins the E prompts on ChatGPT and Gemini, which is where her depth is.
6. **Cybersecurity is a different arena.** None of the six is named in D answers; do not benchmark cyber PR visibility against them.

## 9. What she should avoid

- Self-crowning "number 1" listicles on head terms with unverifiable superlatives ("most awarded", "600%+ LLM visibility", "only agency with proprietary AI SEO"). Engines and roundup tools discount them and name the issue in the answer text.
- Paid or syndicated roundups that present sponsored rankings as independent reviews (the MEXC news pattern). Claude flags that pattern to users.
- Mass lastmod regeneration or date stamps that do not follow real edits (5W: 1,572 URLs re-dated in October; Coinbound: June 5 against April 2026 on one page and a 2024 heading in a 2026 post).
- HTML where a text file is expected: llms-full.txt or markdown routes that return an HTML error or SPA shell.
- Huge generated artefacts and thousands of FAQ-pattern URLs as a strategy: 5W (7.1 MB llms-full.txt, 411 FAQ-pattern URLs) is named in 4 cells; Guava's 22-URL FAQ sitemap has produced none.
- Unsourced factual claims about named competitors (the Wachsman client and office list, section 4.5). Add a dated source for each or remove it.
- Treating the panel as proof. It is a 92-cell, single-run sample built around her positioning. Head-term and cyber visibility need a larger, independently designed prompt set.
