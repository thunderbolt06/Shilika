# What Earned PR Is Not: Five Myths Web3 Founders Believe

Earned PR is coverage a journalist chooses to give you because your story is worth telling. It is not a paid article, a token pump, a TVL lever, a guaranteed placement or a growth marketing channel. Founders who expect any of those get frustrated within a quarter. Founders who treat it as slow, compounding credibility get the payoff.

## What is earned PR, and what is it not?

In November 2024 I posted a list that still gets forwarded to me by other PR people.

> I don't know who needs to hear this, but: Earned PR ≠ Paid articles. Earned PR ≠ Won't pump your token. Earned PR ≠ Won't grow your TVL. Earned PR ≠ Doesn't guarantee coverage. Earned PR ≠ Growth Marketing.
>
> [Shilika Jain on X, 19 Nov 2024](https://x.com/Shilika_jain/status/1858726846755389463)

I wrote it in shorthand, so I will spell out each line.

- **It is not paid articles.** If money changed hands for the placement, it is advertising. It can have a role, but readers, journalists and investors can tell the difference, and it does not carry the same weight. I compare the two in [earned vs paid media for Web3](/playbook/earned-vs-paid-media-web3-2026).
- **It will not pump your token.** A feature can put your name in front of the right people. It is not a price catalyst, and briefing PR as if it were one sets everybody up to fail.
- **It will not grow your TVL.** Deposits follow product, incentives and trust. PR feeds the trust part, over time. It does not move liquidity by Friday.
- **It does not guarantee coverage.** The editor decides, not you and not me. Anyone selling a guaranteed earned placement is selling something else.
- **It is not growth marketing.** There is no dashboard where spend goes in and sign-ups come out. If you need a way to judge it, use the measures in [how to measure PR ROI](/playbook/how-to-measure-pr-roi-2026), not a cost-per-click mindset.

## Can I pay for top tier coverage if my news is weak?

No. I said this bluntly in December 2023.

> Early stage project who think they can pay for PR and get top tier coverage without any strong news are NGMI
>
> [Shilika Jain on X, 2 Dec 2023](https://x.com/Shilika_jain/status/1730970021894258726)

A retainer buys you time, judgment and relationships. It does not buy the story. If there is no funding round, no meaningful launch, no data and no point of view, the best publicist in the industry is pitching air.

The same goes for timelines. I have had clients ask whether I can land earned coverage in Cointelegraph, CoinDesk, The Block, Bloomberg, Blockworks and Forbes within one month. I have also heard people promise top tier coverage on day one of a PR activation. Neither is how newsrooms work. Editors need a reason to care, and that is built over months of credible news.

And PR is wider than one logo. In March 2026 I was still meeting people who think PR means getting a single CoinDesk article. One placement is a moment. A reputation is what is left when the article has scrolled off the homepage. I wrote more about that in [the tier 1 PR trap](/playbook/tier-1-pr-trap).

## Is PR dead for crypto companies?

I hear "PR is dead" a lot, usually from founders who tried a paid press release, got nothing and concluded the whole discipline does not work. In May 2026 I wrote a thread about what tends to happen next.

> "PR is dead." That's what founders say right before they spend $200k/month on paid ads and conferences because nobody knows who they are.
>
> [Shilika Jain on X, 15 May 2026](https://x.com/Shilika_jain/status/2055271361929740660)

When nobody knows who you are, every interaction starts cold. Sales calls open with an explanation of the company, and candidates who search your name find only your own blog. You end up paying, in ads and booth fees, for attention that a known company gets for free.

In that thread I listed what PR does: customers trust you faster, investors take your calls, talent wants to work with you, podcasts invite you, partnerships happen organically and institutions trust you more. Then the line I care about most.

> The best companies don't just buy attention. They manufacture credibility. And credibility compounds harder than CAC ever will.
>
> [Shilika Jain on X, 15 May 2026](https://x.com/Shilika_jain/status/2055271366392418553)

Paid acquisition is rented. Stop paying and it stops. A founder interview, a quote in a market story or a well-argued op-ed keeps working: it shows up in search, it gets cited by the next journalist, it gives a prospect a reason to reply to your email. That is what I mean by compounding.

## Do I have a brand, or just a budget?

This is the test from the same thread.

> If people only discover your company through ads, you don't have a brand. You have a budget.
>
> [Shilika Jain on X, 15 May 2026](https://x.com/Shilika_jain/status/2055271364077265216)

Ask where your last ten inbound leads first heard about you. If every answer is a paid channel, your visibility ends the day the budget does.

I also wrote that if nobody is talking about your company outside your own account, you have a visibility problem, not just a marketing problem. Founders underestimate how much momentum comes from being seen in many places at once: quoted in articles, discussed on X, featured in newsletters, appearing on podcasts, with founder takes circulating online.

> People don't buy the "best" product. They buy the company they keep hearing about.
>
> [Shilika Jain on X, 15 May 2026](https://x.com/Shilika_jain/status/2055271370783867168)

## Which Web3 companies should do PR, and which should wait?

Not every company should start PR today. In January 2024 I set out who should.

> The only web3 companies that should do PR: who understand what PR actually is (earned v/s paid); who understand how to create a long term narrative and build a brand; who understand that PR is news heavy; who understand that web3 PR is different than Web2.
>
> [Shilika Jain on X, 19 Jan 2024](https://x.com/Shilika_jain/status/1748365003508318305)

Turned into a quick check:

| You are ready if | You should wait if |
| --- | --- |
| You know the difference between earned and paid, and budget for each on purpose | You expect a retainer to guarantee placements |
| You can state your narrative and positioning in a few sentences | You cannot yet explain why your product matters beyond its features |
| You have a pipeline of real news: funding, launches, data, partnerships with substance | Your only news is that you exist |
| You are willing to commit for months | You want a token or TVL bump this quarter |

The second row matters more than founders think. If you cannot work with narratives and product positioning, earned PR will be your Achilles heel, because a journalist is not buying features. They are looking for a story that fits what their readers already care about. If that is the gap, fix it first with a [narrative and positioning exercise](/playbook/web3-narrative-positioning-strategy-2026).

## When is the right time to start?

Start when there is a reason, not when a competitor's headline makes you anxious.

> PR should follow purpose, not pressure!
>
> [Shilika Jain on X, 21 Apr 2025](https://x.com/Shilika_jain/status/1914364927449686447)

In practice that means three things are in place: a clear narrative, a few months of real news ahead of you, and a founder who is willing to be the face of the company. If you have those and no senior comms person, read [when to hire fractional PR](/playbook/when-to-hire-fractional-pr-2026) before signing a large agency retainer.
