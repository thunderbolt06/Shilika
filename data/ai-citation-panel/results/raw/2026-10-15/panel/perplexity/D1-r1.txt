PROMPT D1 | Who are the best PR firms for cybersecurity startups in 2026? | run 1 | surface: Perplexity (Search mode, default model, Incognito on) | captured 2026-10-16
ANSWER:
The strongest PR choices for cybersecurity startups in 2026 are security-native specialists, especially Merritt Group, 10Fold Communications, Montner Tech PR, Eskenzi PR, and Bora, with larger firms such as Highwire, Inkhouse, Mission North, and Weber Shandwick better suited once you need scale or multi-market programs. The right pick depends most on your stage, geography, and whether you need launch PR, analyst relations, technical thought leadership, or crisis support.
[cite: securityboulevard +1]

Best fit by startup stage
Firm | Best for | Why it stands out
Merritt Group | Seed-to-growth startups selling commercial and/or federal | Dedicated cybersecurity practice; has supported 80+ security clients including CrowdStrike, Venafi, and Black Hat; strong federal/public-sector reach. [securityboulevard]
10Fold Communications | Growth-stage vendors preparing for funding, acquisition, or IPO | Long-established cyber practice; clients include Exabeam, Swimlane, Tuskira, and Retym; combines PR, analyst relations, and measurable business outcomes. [securityboulevard]
Montner Tech PR | Early-stage and Israeli cybersecurity startups | More than 20 years of cybersecurity focus and a close relationship with YL Ventures and its portfolio, particularly useful for stealth-to-funding visibility. [securityboulevard]
Eskenzi PR | UK/European market entry and security-trade coverage | One of the longest-standing cybersecurity-focused PR firms, with deep security-media relationships, especially in the UK and Europe. [analyticsinsight]
Bora | Technical thought leadership and content-led PR | Works exclusively on cybersecurity content and thought leadership; team includes former practitioners and security journalists, with clients including Cisco, Thales, and Venafi. [securityboulevard]
PRLab | Earlier-stage startups wanting a defined, accessible program | Offers a dedicated cybersecurity PR line and startup-oriented B2B communications rather than an enterprise-only model. [analyticsinsight]
Code Red Communications | Founders wanting a small, hands-on specialist team | Security-focused positioning across media, social, and analyst relations; better for senior attention than global scale. [analyticsinsight]
Highwire PR | Later-stage cybersecurity companies | Strong enterprise software, AI, cloud, and cybersecurity roster; better suited to growth and late-stage programs than a first launch. [beantownmv +1]
Weber Shandwick | Funded startups expanding internationally | Global reach and integrated campaign infrastructure, though smaller startups may receive less day-to-day senior attention. [analyticsinsight]

Strong alternatives
Corporate Ink: Good for B2B security companies whose story overlaps with broader enterprise IT, AI, and cloud; emphasizes positioning and steady media relations. [analyticsinsight]
RH Strategic: A solid choice if you sell into government, regulated industries, or public-sector-adjacent markets. [analyticsinsight]
CyberTheory: More of a marketing/advisory partner than a pure PR shop, but valuable if CISO intent data and demand generation matter alongside visibility. [securityboulevard]
Emberline Founder PR: Worth evaluating for very early founders focused on personal brand and executive visibility, particularly around the $1M-$10M ARR range. [secureblitz]

How to choose
For a typical cybersecurity startup, I would shortlist Merritt Group, 10Fold, and Montner first for U.S. launch and growth PR; add Eskenzi if Europe is material. If your differentiation is deep technical research, Bora is especially compelling; if you need federal credibility, prioritize Merritt or RH Strategic. [securityboulevard +1]

Before signing, ask each agency for:
- Named cybersecurity clients at a similar stage and the coverage they earned.
- The specific security reporters, podcasts, newsletters, and analysts they would target.
- Examples of translating technical research, threat intelligence, or product launches into credible stories.
- A crisis-communications plan for a breach, vulnerability disclosure, or executive controversy.
- Who exactly will run the account, not just who pitches it.

Cybersecurity PR works best when the agency can speak credibly to skeptical CISOs and security journalists rather than relying on generic B2B hype; specialist firms generally provide faster relevance and stronger trade-media relationships, while global firms add scale once the program becomes multi-region or enterprise-wide.
