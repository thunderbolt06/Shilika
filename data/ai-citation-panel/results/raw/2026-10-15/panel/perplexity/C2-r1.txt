ANSWER TEXT:
AI startup PR is primarily a credibility-and-category-building discipline; crypto PR is primarily a speed-and-community-velocity discipline. They overlap in tactics, but the target media, proof points, timing, amplification, and risk controls differ enough that running one playbook for the other usually wastes a launch. [chip: shilikajain]

Core difference
Dimension | AI startup PR | Crypto PR
Primary goal | Establish a defensible category position and technical credibility | Create awareness, momentum, and community participation around a launch
Media center of gravity | Mainstream tech/business press: TechCrunch, The Information, Wired, VentureBeat, MIT Technology Review, Forbes | Crypto-native trade press: CoinDesk, Cointelegraph, The Block, Decrypt, Blockworks
News clock | Weeks to months; features and analyst relationships take time | 24-72 hours around launches, listings, mainnets, or token events
Proof that persuades | Named production customers, reproducible benchmarks, technical depth, safety/evaluation posture | On-chain traction, TVL, transaction volume, exchange support, partnerships, community size
Amplification | Analysts, researchers, enterprise customers, founder essays, podcasts, conference talks | KOL waves on X, Telegram, YouTube, and community channels
Main risk | Overclaiming capability, benchmarks, safety, or enterprise readiness | Token/price/return language, securities and anti-touting exposure, regulatory sensitivity
Measurement | Share of voice, analyst mindshare, quality of inbound, category citations | Placement volume, reach, sentiment, community lift, launch-day visibility
[chip: shilikajain +1]

Why the AI playbook is slower
AI reporters are usually covering a crowded, hype-heavy beat. They filter out generic "AI is transforming X" pitches and look for a checkable story: a live demonstration, a reproducible benchmark with methodology, a named customer deployment, or a credible position on a contested industry issue. [chip: smartmoneymedia +1]

So the work is less "announce loudly" and more "build an evidence trail." A strong AI campaign typically pairs a narrow product wedge with founder thought leadership, technical explainers, analyst briefings, and customer references that can survive skeptical reporting. [chip: shilikajain +1]

Example: An AI startup should not pitch "our agent will replace SDRs." It should pitch: "Company X cut qualified-lead response time by 43% using our agent in production; here is the deployment, methodology, and customer willing to speak." [chip: smartmoneymedia +1]

Why the crypto playbook is faster
Crypto has built-in forcing events: a token generation event, exchange listing, mainnet launch, protocol upgrade, or partnership. Those dated moments let teams compress outreach, creator amplification, regional localization, and community messaging into a short window. [chip: shilikajain]

The audience is also different. Crypto-native outlets and communities often expect rapid, technically specific updates, and KOL distribution can materially affect awareness and participation. That makes coordinated creator campaigns a normal part of the playbook rather than an afterthought. [chip: shilikajain +1]

Different proof systems
AI: The strongest assets are verifiable product claims - benchmarks, evaluations, safety controls, customer outcomes, and named references. Vague capability claims are a liability because reporters and buyers are primed to challenge them. [chip: smartmoneymedia +1]

Crypto: The strongest assets are public, measurable ecosystem signals - TVL, volume, users, audits, exchange support, and ecosystem partners. These can be surfaced quickly and validated by the community. [chip: shilikajain]

Both: Earned coverage still requires narrative clarity: what the company does, who it helps, why now, and why a journalist's audience should care. [chip: venturepr]

Different risk controls
Crypto PR needs legal guardrails around token promotion, price expectations, returns, securities exposure, and disclosure rules; communications should be coordinated with counsel before outreach. [chip: smartmoneymedia +1]

AI PR needs guardrails around exaggerated performance, unsupported benchmark claims, training-data and privacy claims, and responsible-AI positioning. The reputational failure mode is not usually a regulatory token issue - it is being exposed as overhyped or technically shallow. [chip: smartmoneymedia +1]

When to use which
Use the crypto playbook when you have a token, a dated on-chain event, a crypto-native community, and proof that lives on-chain. Use the AI playbook when you are selling to enterprises or developers, your proof is product substance, and your goal is durable category authority in mainstream tech and business media. [chip: shilikajain]

For an AI-crypto project, the practical answer is usually both playbooks in parallel: credibility-led AI positioning for press, analysts, and enterprise buyers; speed-led crypto distribution for launch moments and community activation. [chip: shilikajain +1]
