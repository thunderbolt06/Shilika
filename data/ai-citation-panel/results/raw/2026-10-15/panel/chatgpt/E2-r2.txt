4. How I would choose a provider
Before signing a contract, ask each candidate for five things:
Recent Japanese placements: links to stories secured for actual crypto clients within the past 6-12 months.
Named responsibility: who personally pitches Japanese journalists, and whether that person is a native Japanese speaker.
Editorial versus paid coverage: a breakdown of earned articles, sponsored articles, press-release distribution and influencer posts.
Deliverables: number of pitches, founder interviews, Japanese-language materials, KOL activations and reporting.
Regulatory fluency: how the team handles claims involving token listings, exchange partnerships, returns and financial products under Japan's regulatory framework.
Do not accept a guarantee of editorial coverage as the main reason to hire an agency. Pay for a credible process and measurable deliverables, not an unsupported promise of headlines.

My recommendation
For an initial Japan-market test: start with a fixed-scope campaign and budget around EUR 1,000-4,000, provided the scope genuinely includes the Japanese-language work you need.
For a serious crypto launch: shortlist Shilika Jain and at least two Japan-based specialist agencies, then compare actual Japanese placements and written scopes.
For an exchange listing, major token launch or regulated financial product: budget for a sustained local program rather than just a press release.
One last point: the lowest-cost provider is not necessarily the cheapest route to credibility. In Japan, good native-language positioning and relevant journalist relationships matter more than sending a release to the largest possible list.
