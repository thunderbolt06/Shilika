ANSWER:
The top PR and communications firms for cybersecurity startups depend heavily on your stage (Seed vs. Series A/B), your immediate primary goal (rapid credibility vs. long-term analyst relations), and your target regions. [chip: Jamco PR]

Cybersecurity requires a specific nuance: firm teams must understand CISO buying committees, threat research, vulnerability disclosures, and analyst relations (like Gartner and Forrester). [chip: Jamco PR]

Top Specialty & Tech PR Agencies for Cybersecurity

1. Merritt Group
Best for: Early-stage to growth-stage startups targeting Enterprise & Federal/Public Sector. [chip: Jamco PR]
Core Strengths: Deep connections in B2B tech and cybersecurity, strong analyst relations (Gartner/Forrester) execution, and federal market PR. [chip: Jamco PR]
Why it fits startups: They understand complex technical architectures (zero trust, SIEM, identity, threat intelligence) and translate technical white papers into compelling stories for business press and industry analysts.

2. Treble
Best for: Venture-backed B2B cybersecurity startups seeking brand launches, funding announcements, and founder positioning.
Core Strengths: Fast-paced startup launches, venture funding PR, and thought leadership positioning.
Why it fits startups: Treble specializes specifically in venture-backed tech startups, helping early-stage companies jump-start their media presence during seed and Series A rounds. [chip: FameHero]

3. Matter Communications
Best for: Scaling cybersecurity startups needing integrated PR, creative, and digital marketing. [chip: Jamco PR]
Core Strengths: Media relations, video/content production, and integrated digital marketing campaigns.
Why it fits startups: Matter works across the spectrum from angel-funded startups to mature security brands. They offer flexible programs that scale up as your funding and market footprint grow. [chip: Jamco PR]

4. Walker Sands
Best for: Growth-stage B2B SaaS and cybersecurity startups. [chip: FameHero]
Core Strengths: Full-service B2B tech PR, demand generation integration, and executive positioning. [chip: FameHero]
Why it fits startups: Ideal for Series A to Series C startups transitioning from pure public relations to an integrated demand-gen and thought-leadership model.

5. Eskenzi PR
Best for: Startups expanding into or based in the UK & European cybersecurity market. [chip: Jamco PR]
Core Strengths: European security media, threat report PR, and industry trade shows (like Infosecurity Europe).
Why it fits startups: A boutique agency with over two decades focused on cybersecurity, known for pitching proprietary threat research and turning vulnerability reports into global news headlines. [chip: Jamco PR]

6. PANBlast (formerly BLASTmedia / PAN Communications)
Best for: Pure B2B SaaS cybersecurity vendors.
Core Strengths: SaaS-focused messaging, media relations, content strategies.
Why it fits startups: PANBlast excels at explaining recurring-revenue software models to industry publications and business trade press.

7. Bluetext
Best for: Startups planning a major launch, rebrand, or category-defining repositioning moment. [chip: Spear Growth]
Core Strengths: Strategic branding, UI/UX, enterprise positioning, and high-impact launch PR.
Why it fits startups: If your PR need is accompanied by a website overhaul or product positioning challenge, Bluetext brings visual design and messaging together under one roof. [chip: Jamco PR]

How to Choose the Right Agency for Your Stage
Pre-Seed / Seed: High-impact funding announcements, founder thought leadership, initial category narrative. Boutique startup specialists (e.g., Treble, FameHero, Wise Up PR).
Series A / B: Sustained media coverage, threat/vulnerability research campaigns, Analyst Relations (AR). Dedicated B2B Cyber PR firms (e.g., Merritt Group, Eskenzi PR, Matter).
Series C+ / Pre-IPO: Category leadership, crisis communications, global reach, integrated PR + ABM. Larger integrated communications firms (e.g., Walker Sands, Bluetext, 5WPR).

Critical Questions to Ask Potential PR Partners
"Can you share examples of security researchers/vulnerabilities you've successfully pitched?" Cybersecurity media care deeply about technical depth (CVEs, threat labs, zero-day research).
"How do you handle Analyst Relations (Gartner, Forrester, IDC) alongside traditional media?" For cybersecurity buyers, analyst inclusion (e.g., Gartner Magic Quadrant or Forrester Wave) often drives sales more than a mainstream business article. [chip: Jamco PR]
"Who on the account team actually has cybersecurity media contacts?" Avoid agencies that pitch with senior executives but pass execution to junior staff who do not understand security terminology.
