The 20 Marketing Channels Playbook

Every channel an early-stage company is told to "be on", reduced to what actually matters: when it fits, the first three moves, the one number to watch, and the mistake that burns the budget. Written for founders in Web3, AI and cybersecurity, useful for any B2B or developer-facing product.

How to use this playbook

  1. Scan the overview matrix and cross out every channel your buyer does not use. Be honest.
  2. Read the sections for what is left. Note which ones you could start this week with the skills you already have.
  3. Use the "Pick 3 by stage" page to choose. Three channels, eight weeks, written kill criteria.
  4. Plan the money in the Marketing Budget Template (it has a line for each of the 20 channels).

CHANNELSOwned and search

01 · SEO

Low cash · 3 to 6 months · Demand capture
When it fits
Buyers search for the problem or the category in words you can name today. Your product has a long consideration cycle and buyers research before talking to sales.
First 3 actions
  1. List 30 questions your buyers type, from sales calls and support tickets, not only keyword tools.
  2. Fix the technical basics: indexation in Search Console, unique titles, internal links from the homepage to money pages.
  3. Publish 4 pages that each answer one high-intent question better than the current top result, with a clear next step.
Metric to watch
Non-branded organic clicks to pages that lead to a signup or demo, plus signups from those pages.
Common mistake
Publishing high-volume top-of-funnel posts ("what is blockchain") that rank for nobody who will ever buy.

02 · GEO / AI search

Low cash · 1 to 3 months · Citation
When it fits
Your buyers ask ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini or Claude for recommendations ("best smart contract auditors", "AI SOC tools for mid-market"). Increasingly, this is most technical B2B.
First 3 actions
  1. Ask 20 buyer questions across four AI assistants and log who gets cited and which sources are linked.
  2. Make your entity consistent: same name, description and founder details on your site, LinkedIn, Crunchbase, GitHub and directories.
  3. Publish answer-first pages with citable paragraphs and FAQs, and earn mentions on the third-party sources the assistants already cite.
Metric to watch
Share of the 20 tracked prompts where you are cited or named, checked monthly.
Common mistake
Treating it as a technical trick (only adding llms.txt or schema). Assistants mostly repeat what credible third parties say about you.

11 · Email

Low cost · Immediate · Conversion and retention
When it fits
Always, once you have a list. It is the only channel you own end to end and the one that turns launch traffic into users.
First 3 actions
  1. Authenticate your sending domain (SPF, DKIM, DMARC) before the first send.
  2. Write a 4-email welcome sequence that drives one activation action, not a newsletter archive.
  3. Ship a monthly product update to everyone, with one story and one call to action.
Metric to watch
Click-to-activation rate from the welcome sequence. Open rates are unreliable since mailbox privacy changes.
Common mistake
Buying lists or cold emailing from the primary domain, which damages deliverability for every email you send after.

CHANNELSPaid

03 · Google Ads

$2K to $5K test · 1 to 2 weeks · Demand capture
When it fits
People already search for your category, a competitor, or the exact problem, and you can afford the click price for that intent.
First 3 actions
  1. Start with exact and phrase match on 10 to 20 high-intent terms plus competitor terms. No broad match until conversions are tracked.
  2. Build one landing page per intent cluster whose headline matches the search.
  3. Import offline conversions (qualified lead, opportunity) from your CRM so bidding optimises for quality.
Metric to watch
Cost per qualified lead, and search terms report weekly to add negatives.
Common mistake
Letting Performance Max or broad match spend the test budget on irrelevant searches before conversion tracking is right. Note: crypto and some financial products need Google certification before ads will serve.

04 · Meta Ads

$2K to $5K test · 1 to 2 weeks · Demand creation
When it fits
Consumer, prosumer or creator products, broad audiences, and retargeting site visitors for any product. Weak for narrow enterprise roles.
First 3 actions
  1. Install the pixel plus Conversions API so events survive browser restrictions.
  2. Produce 6 to 10 creative variations across 3 angles (problem, outcome, social proof). Creative is the targeting now.
  3. Run one broad campaign and one retargeting campaign. Keep structure simple so the algorithm can learn.
Metric to watch
Cost per activated user (not cost per signup), by creative angle.
Common mistake
Judging the channel after 3 days or on one creative. Also: crypto ads require prior written permission from Meta in most cases.

05 · LinkedIn Ads

$3K to $8K test · 2 to 4 weeks · Precise B2B reach
When it fits
Your average contract value is high enough to absorb expensive clicks, and you can describe the buyer by title, function, seniority and company size.
First 3 actions
  1. Upload a named-account list (your ICP accounts) and layer job function and seniority.
  2. Run Thought Leader Ads that promote the founder's best organic posts. They tend to beat polished brand creative for early-stage companies.
  3. Offer something worth a work email: a benchmark, a template, a teardown. Not "book a demo" to cold audiences.
Metric to watch
Cost per qualified lead from target accounts, and engagement from named accounts.
Common mistake
Audiences too broad, budget too thin. Ten dollars a day across five campaigns teaches you nothing.

CHANNELSSocial and communities

06 · Reddit

Time · 2 to 6 weeks · Credibility
When it fits
There are active subreddits where your buyers ask for advice (developer tools, security, crypto, specific ecosystems). Reddit threads are also frequently cited by AI assistants and Google.
First 3 actions
  1. Pick 3 to 5 subreddits and read each subreddit's rules on self-promotion.
  2. Have the founder or an engineer answer questions for a month under a real, disclosed identity, with no links unless asked.
  3. When you share your own work, share the useful thing (the data, the write-up), and disclose that you built it.
Metric to watch
Referral sessions and self-reported "Reddit" on signup forms; upvoted helpful comments as a leading signal.
Common mistake
Astroturfing with alt accounts or paid upvotes. Communities spot it fast and the thread becomes the top search result about you.

07 · X / Twitter

Time · 2 to 8 weeks · Narrative
When it fits
Crypto, AI and developer audiences where news, launches and arguments still break first on X. Journalists in these beats watch it closely.
First 3 actions
  1. Founder account first, company account second. Rewrite the bio to say what you build and for whom.
  2. Post daily for 6 weeks: one original insight, the rest replies to the 30 accounts your buyers follow.
  3. Turn each launch or data point into a thread with a visual and one clear link at the end.
Metric to watch
Profile visits and follows from your target audience; inbound DMs from buyers, partners or press.
Common mistake
Engagement-bait and giveaway follows that inflate counts with accounts that will never buy or cover you.

08 · LinkedIn organic

Time · 4 to 8 weeks · Founder-led trust
When it fits
B2B, enterprise, security and AI infrastructure, where buyers, investors and hires all check the founder's profile before replying.
First 3 actions
  1. Fix the founder profile: headline that names the customer and outcome, featured section with the best proof.
  2. Post 3 times a week using the 30-Day Founder Content Calendar. Lessons, opinions with evidence, behind-the-scenes.
  3. Spend 20 minutes a day leaving substantive comments on posts by target buyers.
Metric to watch
Profile views from ICP titles and inbound conversations started, not likes.
Common mistake
Company-page-only posting. People follow people; company pages get a fraction of the reach.

13 · Communities (Discord, Slack, Telegram)

Time · 1 to 3 months · Retention and advocacy
When it fits
Your users benefit from talking to each other: developer tools, open source, protocols, Web3 products with token holders. Not every SaaS needs its own community.
First 3 actions
  1. Pick one platform where your users already are. Telegram for many crypto audiences, Discord for developers and gaming, Slack for B2B practitioners.
  2. Seed it with 30 to 50 real users before announcing it, and run a weekly programme (office hours, changelog, AMA).
  3. Lock down security: verified admin roles, no DMs from staff by default, pinned anti-scam notice.
Metric to watch
Weekly active members who post, and questions answered by members instead of staff.
Common mistake
Launching an empty server, or counting members inflated by airdrop hunters as community.

CHANNELSVideo and messaging

09 · YouTube

Medium · 3 to 6 months · Education and search
When it fits
Your product needs showing, not telling, and buyers look for tutorials, comparisons and walkthroughs. YouTube is also the second-largest search surface and is cited by AI assistants.
First 3 actions
  1. Record 5 videos that answer the most common "how do I" questions from support and sales.
  2. Write titles the way people search them, and add chapters and a full transcript.
  3. Embed each video on the matching docs or blog page.
Metric to watch
Average view duration on tutorial videos and clicks to your site from descriptions.
Common mistake
Uploading webinar recordings with no edit and calling it a channel.

10 · TikTok / Shorts / Reels

Low to medium · 2 to 8 weeks · Reach
When it fits
Consumer products, consumer AI apps, creator tools, and employer brand. Works for B2B only when a founder is genuinely good on camera.
First 3 actions
  1. Watch 50 videos in your niche and note the formats that hold attention in the first 2 seconds.
  2. Batch-record 12 videos in one session: hook, one idea, one visual payoff, under 45 seconds.
  3. Post the same cuts to TikTok, YouTube Shorts and Instagram Reels and keep what travels.
Metric to watch
Average watch time and share rate per video; profile clicks.
Common mistake
Repurposing polished ads into the feed. Native, lo-fi and specific outperforms brand-safe and generic.

12 · WhatsApp

Low · 1 to 4 weeks · High-touch nurture
When it fits
Your buyers live on WhatsApp for business: India, the Gulf, LATAM, Africa and much of Southeast Asia. Strong for event follow-up, community broadcasts and founder-led sales.
First 3 actions
  1. Set up WhatsApp Business with a verified profile, catalogue or service list, and quick replies.
  2. Collect explicit opt-in (event QR codes, website click-to-chat). Never add people to groups without consent.
  3. Use a Channel or broadcast list for updates and keep 1:1 chats for sales conversations.
Metric to watch
Reply rate and meetings booked from WhatsApp conversations.
Common mistake
Treating it like email blast. Frequent unsolicited messages get you reported and restricted.

14 · Product Hunt and launch sites

Low · Launch day · Spike and backlinks
When it fits
Developer tools, AI apps and prosumer SaaS with a self-serve signup. Less useful for enterprise-only or token launches.
First 3 actions
  1. Prepare assets 2 weeks out: tagline, gallery, 60-second video, maker's first comment with the story and an offer.
  2. Tell your existing users and community the day before, and ask for honest feedback rather than upvotes.
  3. Cross-post the same week to Show HN (if technical), BetaList, relevant subreddits and niche directories.
Metric to watch
Signups and activated users from launch traffic, not the leaderboard rank.
Common mistake
Asking for upvotes in mass DMs or vote rings. It breaks platform rules and can get the launch penalised.

CHANNELSEarned and borrowed attention

15 · PR / earned media

Time or $5K to $12K/mo · 4 to 12 weeks · Credibility
When it fits
You have real news (funding, launch, data, a notable customer, a strong expert view) and trust is part of the purchase. In Web3, AI and security it often is the purchase.
First 3 actions
  1. Build a 30-name media list by beat with one recent relevant article per reporter.
  2. Write a one-page angle memo: why this matters to their readers this month, what is new, what proof you have.
  3. Offer one reporter an exclusive or a small group an embargo, with a founder available for interviews.
Metric to watch
Pieces in target outlets that mention your positioning correctly, plus referral traffic and AI citations that follow.
Common mistake
Mass-sending a press release to hundreds of contacts. Journalists cover stories, not announcements.

16 · Podcasts

Time · 1 to 3 months · Depth
When it fits
The founder can hold a 45-minute conversation with specific stories and opinions. Niche shows with engaged audiences beat big generic ones.
First 3 actions
  1. List 25 shows your buyers listen to (ask customers directly).
  2. Pitch each host with 3 episode ideas tailored to their recent episodes, not a founder bio.
  3. Clip every appearance into 3 short videos and a written recap for your own channels.
Metric to watch
Appearances per month and self-reported "heard you on a podcast" in signups.
Common mistake
Paying for "guaranteed placement" packages on shows no buyer listens to.

17 · Influencers / KOLs

Varies widely · 1 to 4 weeks · Borrowed trust
When it fits
Crypto launches, consumer AI and developer tools where a handful of voices shape what an audience tries next.
First 3 actions
  1. Vet audiences, not follower counts: check reply quality, past sponsored posts and how those projects fared.
  2. Write a brief with facts, required disclosures and things they must not say, then let them use their own voice.
  3. Stagger posts in waves instead of one coordinated blast that reads as paid.
Metric to watch
Tracked signups or wallet connections per creator, via unique links or codes.
Common mistake
Undisclosed paid promotion. It is a regulatory risk in many markets and a reputational one everywhere.

CHANNELSPartners, events and referrals

18 · Partnerships and integrations

Time · 2 to 6 months · Distribution
When it fits
Your product sits next to a platform your buyers already use (a cloud marketplace, a chain ecosystem, a SIEM, an LLM platform).
First 3 actions
  1. List the 10 tools your best customers use alongside yours.
  2. Build or polish one integration and get listed in that partner's marketplace or ecosystem page.
  3. Propose one co-marketing moment: joint webinar, co-authored guide, or launch announcement.
Metric to watch
Sourced and influenced pipeline from partner listings and joint campaigns.
Common mistake
Signing ten logo-swap partnerships with no shared customer and no follow-through.

19 · Events and conferences

$3K to $50K+ · Event week · Pipeline
When it fits
Enterprise, security and Web3, where key buyers, investors and reporters are all in one city for a week.
First 3 actions
  1. Book 15 to 25 meetings before you arrive. The booth is optional; the calendar is not.
  2. Host something small and specific (a 20-person dinner, a side event) instead of sponsoring the main stage.
  3. Follow up within 48 hours with notes from the conversation, not a generic deck.
Metric to watch
Qualified meetings held and pipeline created per dollar, tracked for 90 days after.
Common mistake
Paying for a booth with no meeting plan and measuring success by badge scans.

20 · Referral programs

Low plus incentives · 1 to 3 months · Compounding
When it fits
Customers already recommend you unprompted. A referral program amplifies word of mouth; it does not create it.
First 3 actions
  1. Ask for the referral at the moment of value (first successful outcome), inside the product.
  2. Give both sides something that relates to the product (credits, seats, a higher tier) rather than cash where possible.
  3. Make sharing one click with a prewritten message the user can edit.
Metric to watch
Share of new signups from referrals and their activation rate versus other channels.
Common mistake
Launching before product-market fit, or rewarding signups instead of activated users, which invites fraud.
