How to get featured in CoinDesk, Cointelegraph, and Decrypt in 2026

If you're trying to get a crypto startup, Web3 project, founder, or token featured in major crypto publications, the key is to give journalists a credible story with independent news value - not simply pitch your company for coverage.

There are several ways to earn visibility: editorial news coverage, founder interviews, expert commentary, contributed articles, podcast appearances, and sponsored content. These routes have different requirements, costs, and levels of editorial independence.

The most effective strategy is to build a media pitch around a genuine development, support it with verifiable evidence, and approach the right journalist with a concise, tailored pitch.

1. Choose the right route to coverage

CoinDesk
Best approached with a story about markets, institutional finance, funding, regulation, tokenization, major deals, or a significant technical development.

These are editorial-fit suggestions, not guarantees of publication.

2. Six steps to getting featured

Step 1 - Find a real news angle
Identify what has changed, why it matters now, and who is affected. Stronger angles include:
A verified funding round or major strategic partnership.
A product launch with measurable adoption.
New data revealing a market trend.
A security incident, regulatory development, or meaningful industry shift.
A credible founder with original insight into a major story.
"We launched our new platform" is generally weaker than "New data shows how this platform is changing stablecoin settlement."

Step 2 - Identify the journalist who covers your subject
Read each publication's recent coverage. Find reporters covering your precise beat, then explain why your story is relevant to their reporting. For CoinDesk, start with its official newsroom masthead [link]. For Cointelegraph, use its editorial contacts [link].

Step 3 - Prepare a journalist-ready press kit
Include:
A concise announcement or story brief.
Verifiable metrics, dates, and original sources.
Founder biography and interview availability.
Product documentation, demo, and relevant visuals.
Security audit details and funding verification, where applicable.
Clear disclosures of token holdings, paid relationships, and other material conflicts.
Make independent verification easy; don't require journalists to rely solely on your claims.

Step 4 - Send a short, personalized pitch
Keep the initial email around 100-180 words. Lead with the news, explain the broader relevance, offer evidence and access to a credible spokesperson, and make the next step easy.

Step 5 - Make yourself useful to the newsroom
Offer quick, factual answers, original data, expert commentary, and access to relevant people. For breaking news, speed and availability matter. Follow up once after a few business days if appropriate, without repeatedly messaging reporters.

Step 6 - Build repeatable media relationships
After coverage, share the published piece accurately, answer follow-up questions, and continue providing useful information between announcements. Become a reliable source rather than contacting journalists only when you need publicity.

3. Ready-to-use media pitch email
Here is a template you can adapt for a genuine announcement or a proposed news story.

Story idea: [specific development] and its impact on [crypto sector]
Connect email
Recipients

Hi [Journalist's name],

A few details that may be useful:
[Verified metric or important fact]
[Independent evidence or documentation]
[Relevant market context]

Our [founder/executive/technical lead] is available to discuss the development, share supporting data, and answer questions.

Would this be relevant to your current coverage? I'm happy to share the supporting materials or arrange an interview.

Best,
[Your name]
[Role and company]
[Website] | [Contact details]

Important: This is a template for editorial outreach, not a guaranteed formula. Don't send the same generic pitch to every journalist. Tailor the subject, news angle, and supporting evidence to each recipient.

4. Earned media versus paid placements
If the objective is guaranteed brand visibility rather than independent reporting, explore the publications' commercial offerings separately.

Route | What it offers | Key consideration
Earned editorial | Independent reporting, interviews, expert quotes | No guarantee of publication
Guest opinion | Your expertise and argument under your byline | Must meet editorial standards; not an advert
Sponsored content | Paid brand visibility or thought leadership | Clearly distinguishable from independent journalism
Press release distribution | Publication of an official announcement in designated sections | Does not guarantee independent news coverage

For example, CoinDesk advertises sponsored analysis, press releases, podcasts, and other brand partnerships through its advertising page [link].
[chip: CoinDesk +1]

Decrypt explicitly states that its guest opinion pieces are not promotional vehicles, and that it expects exclusive submissions supported by facts and sources.
[chip: Decrypt]

Be cautious of agencies promising guaranteed editorial articles, undisclosed paid placements, or access to journalists in exchange for questionable fees. Verify the exact deliverable and disclosure terms before paying.

5. A practical 30-day outreach plan
Days 1-7 - Build the story: Identify two or three defensible angles, gather evidence, prepare a press kit, and select relevant journalists at all three publications.
Days 8-14 - Pitch selectively: Send tailored pitches to the best-fit journalists. Offer interviews, exclusive data, or advance access where genuinely appropriate.
Days 15-21 - Follow up and contribute: Respond promptly, send one concise follow-up where appropriate, and consider a distinct guest opinion pitch if you have a defensible thesis.
Days 22-30 - Measure and improve: Track replies, interviews, published mentions, referral traffic, backlinks, and relevant business outcomes. Refine your next pitch based on the response.

This is a suggested workflow, not a prediction of how quickly any publication will respond or publish.
