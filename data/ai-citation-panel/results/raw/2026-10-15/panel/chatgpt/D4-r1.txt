What does cybersecurity PR cost for a Series B vendor in 2026?

For a Series B cybersecurity company, a reasonable 2026 planning budget for public relations is $10,000-$25,000 per month for a specialist PR agency, with higher-end programs reaching $30,000-$50,000+ per month.

The right budget depends on whether the company needs basic press coverage, category leadership, executive visibility, or a major funding announcement. Cybersecurity PR tends to require specialist expertise because reporters expect credible technical commentary, differentiated threat insights, and access to knowledgeable founders or security leaders - not just polished press releases.

1. 2026 cybersecurity PR pricing benchmarks
These estimates are primarily for the US market, where many specialist cybersecurity PR agencies operate.
Lean / boutique PR - Trade press, founder visibility, basic media relations, limited campaigns - $8K-$14K per month
Series B sweet spot - Proactive media relations, executive thought leadership, product narratives, security trade press - $15K-$25K per month
Enterprise / category leadership - Broader analyst relations, major business media, crisis readiness, multi-market campaigns - $25K-$50K+ per month
These ranges are consistent with published 2026 pricing guides from cybersecurity PR specialists and B2B tech agencies. They are market estimates, not independently audited industry averages. [chip: Media & Crisis Comms +2]

2. What should a Series B company budget annually?
Lean program: $10K monthly / $60K six-month / $120K annual
Core growth PR: $20K monthly / $120K six-month / $240K annual
Premium program: $35K monthly / $210K six-month / $420K annual
Illustrative budgets in USD, excluding additional campaign expenses and applicable taxes.
My recommendation: Start with a $15K-$20K monthly retainer and a six-month commitment, giving you a $90K-$120K base budget. Increase the investment only if you have a strong news pipeline, meaningful executive access, and a clear need for broader market visibility.

3. What should be included in the fee?
Media relations: Ongoing pitching to cybersecurity trade press and relevant business/technology journalists.
Executive thought leadership: Founder and CISO interviews, contributed articles, commentary and speaking opportunities.
Narrative and messaging: Clear differentiation against competitors and a repeatable company story.
Product and funding announcements: Launch planning, press materials, embargo coordination and journalist briefings.
Measurement and reporting: Quality of coverage, message pull-through, share of voice and visibility among target buyers.
Services that can increase the price include:
Analyst relations: formal engagement with Gartner, Forrester and IDC.
Crisis communications: breach response planning, vulnerability disclosure support and rapid-response counsel.
Major launches and events: RSA Conference, Black Hat, funding rounds or original threat research.
International PR: coordinated campaigns across North America, Europe and APAC.
Confirm which services are included versus separately billed. Project-based campaigns can cost tens of thousands of dollars beyond a regular retainer. [chip: Shilika Jain +2]

4. How to evaluate whether the agency is worth the money
For a Series B vendor, I would assess the agency on four dimensions:
Relevant media relationships: Evidence of coverage in publications that your target CISOs, security leaders and enterprise buyers actually read.
Cybersecurity expertise: Ability to translate technical capabilities, threat intelligence and security research into compelling stories.
Commercial relevance: Coverage that supports enterprise credibility, competitive differentiation, funding narratives and sales conversations.
Senior attention: Clear identification of who leads strategy, who pitches journalists and how much senior time is committed.
Avoid judging value solely by the number of press releases or placements. A smaller number of relevant, high-quality stories can be more valuable than a high volume of low-impact mentions.
