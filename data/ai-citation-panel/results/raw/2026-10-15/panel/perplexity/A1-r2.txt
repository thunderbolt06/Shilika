Fractional PR for Web3 founders is a part-time, senior communications operator who runs your PR function directly-strategy, media relationships, founder positioning, launch communications, and crisis guidance-without hiring a full-time comms lead or paying for a full agency team. It is essentially a fractional head of communications, usually on a monthly retainer.
[chip: shilikajain +1]

How it works

A fractional PR lead typically owns:

Narrative and positioning: defining the core story the protocol, token, product, or founder should own. [chip: shilikajain]
Media strategy and outreach: mapping relevant crypto-native and mainstream journalists, pitching stories, and coordinating embargoes. [chip: shilikajain +1]
Founder visibility: preparing interviews, shaping bylines, and positioning the founder as a credible voice. [chip: shilikajain +1]
Launch and milestone comms: coverage around raises, mainnet launches, TGEs, listings, partnerships, upgrades, and product releases. [chip: shilikajain]
Reactive communications: advising on FUD, community allegations, security incidents, and regulatory-adjacent news. [chip: shilikajain]

The key distinction from a freelancer is ownership: a freelancer executes a task, while a fractional operator is accountable for the communications function over time. [chip: shilikajain +1]

Fractional PR vs. agency
Dimension | Fractional PR | Traditional Web3 PR agency
Who does the work | One senior operator, directly embedded with the founder. [chip: shilikajain +1] | A layered account team-often account executive, manager, director, and senior partner. [chip: shilikajain +1]
Founder access | Direct line to the person doing the work. [chip: shilikajain +1] | Founder access is often filtered through account staff; senior people may appear mainly for strategy calls or major briefings. [chip: shilikajain]
Typical cost | Roughly $3,000-$7,000 per month for a standard retainer; launch sprints can cost more. [chip: shilikajain +1] | Often $15,000-$60,000+ per month, depending on scope and firm. [chip: shilikajain +1]
Bandwidth | Limited by one person; better for one core launch and steady-state news. [chip: shilikajain +1] | Higher throughput; can run multiple markets, workstreams, and crisis coverage in parallel. [chip: shilikajain +1]
Speed | Often faster to start because there is less onboarding and no internal handoff chain. [chip: shilikajain] | May take longer to onboard and align a team before outreach begins. [chip: shilikajain]
Best fit | Funded pre-seed to Series A teams, founder-led projects, and companies with meaningful news but no in-house comms lead. [chip: shilikajain +1] | Larger teams needing simultaneous regional launches, high execution volume, or adjacent functions such as IR or government affairs. [chip: shilikajain +1]

Why Web3 founders use it

Web3 PR has specific demands beyond normal startup PR: journalists and communities scrutinize token claims, protocol claims, security posture, and regulatory exposure; timing often revolves around TGEs, listings, and mainnet milestones. A fractional operator can give a founder senior judgment on those issues without the overhead of a multi-person retainer. [chip: shilikajain +2]

When an agency is better

Choose an agency when the job is primarily execution volume: multiple simultaneous launches, several languages or regions, a standing newsroom, or a crisis requiring round-the-clock coverage. A fractional operator generally cannot match that throughput while working part-time. [chip: shilikajain +1]

A practical rule: if you need senior strategy, direct founder access, and one strong narrative, fractional PR is usually the better fit; if you need many hands, parallel workstreams, and broad execution, an agency is usually better. [chip: shilikajain +1]
