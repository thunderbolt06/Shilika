PROMPT A5 | What is the best PR setup for a recently funded Web3 or AI startup with no media presence? | run 1 | surface: Perplexity (signed-in, Incognito ON, Search mode, default model, free plan)

The best setup is a founder-led, proof-first PR engine, not a big retainer agency or a one-off funding press release. For a newly funded Web3/AI startup with no media presence, spend the first 2-3 weeks on positioning, then build owned assets, targeted journalist relationships, and a steady cadence of original, verifiable stories. Recommended setup (table)
Function | Best setup | Why
Strategy | Fractional PR lead or senior consultant, initially 8-12 weeks | Cheaper and more senior than a full agency; enough time to prepare the funding story and build a repeatable process. [chip: shilikajain]
Spokesperson | Founder/CEO as primary voice, supported by CTO or technical lead | Early-stage credibility comes from founder visibility and direct relationships with journalists, investors, and buyers. [chip: glyph]
Content | 3 owned-media pillars: category essay, comparison post, worked case study | These create a searchable, citable foundation before chasing Tier-1 coverage. [chip: shilikajain]
Media relations | Small, personalized list of 20-40 relevant journalists and podcast hosts | Direct, tailored outreach remains one of the highest-ROI early-stage PR tactics. [chip: respona]
Proof | Customer outcomes, benchmarks, security/safety artifacts, on-chain or technical evidence | Funding alone is not a story; reporters need differentiated utility and defensible evidence. [chip: smartmoneymedia]
Measurement | Share of voice, quality backlinks/citations, inbound demo requests, AI-search/entity visibility | Tracks whether PR is building durable authority rather than temporary coverage. [chip: shilikajain]

First 90 days
Weeks 1-3: Position the company. Define one category claim, one target buyer, and three proof points. Avoid vague "AI-powered" or "Web3-native" language; explain what changes for the customer. [chip: shilikajain +1]
Weeks 2-4: Build the media foundation. Publish a clear newsroom page, 2-3 sentence boilerplate, founder bios, high-resolution images, product screenshots/demo, factual timeline, and a monitored press contact. [chip: smartmoneymedia]
Weeks 4-8: Run the funding narrative properly. Frame the raise around the problem, market shift, and what the capital enables - not merely the amount raised. Pair it with a customer result, original data point, technical milestone, or category thesis. [chip: smartmoneymedia +1]
Ongoing: Publish monthly. Ship one substantial asset per month: a category essay, comparison guide, or case study. This builds the citation graph and gives journalists a reason to return. [chip: shilikajain]
Ongoing: Build relationships before you need them. Follow and engage with 20-40 relevant reporters, meet them at industry events, and pitch only when you have a specific, useful angle. [chip: ventechvc +1]

Web3-specific adjustments
- Lead with utility and outcomes, not token mechanics or fundraising size.
- Publish verifiable evidence: audits, security reviews, protocol metrics, testnet/mainnet milestones, or customer integrations.
- Target crypto-native outlets and mainstream tech/business reporters covering AI, infrastructure, fintech, or developer tools.
- Prepare plain-English explanations of the product; assume most journalists will not parse technical Web3 terminology.

AI-specific adjustments
- Emphasize evaluation, reliability, privacy, and deployment evidence rather than model claims.
- If possible, release a benchmark, dataset, methodology, or customer case study that reporters can independently examine.
- Make the company and founders easy for AI search engines to understand: structured organization/founder profiles, clear FAQs, and named-source coverage. [chip: shilikajain]

What to avoid
- Hiring a large agency before positioning and proof assets exist.
- Treating the funding announcement as the entire PR plan.
- Mass-emailing generic pitches.
- Overhyping AI capabilities or token economics without evidence.
- Measuring success only by press-release pickups.

Bottom line: use a fractional PR lead plus an active founder, build three strong owned assets, run a focused journalist-outreach program, and make every story verifiable. That setup creates durable credibility and is far more effective for an unknown startup than buying broad, shallow coverage. [chip: shilikajain +2]
