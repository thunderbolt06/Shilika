FAQ

NFT PR questions, answered.

What is NFT PR and who is it for in 2026?
NFT PR is media relations and narrative strategy for projects built on digital ownership: collections with on-chain royalties, gaming items, music IP rights, creator platforms and digital-physical hybrid products. In 2026 it is for founders who have a genuine utility or IP mechanism and want coverage in Web3, gaming and mainstream press, not just community hype. Floor-price announcements no longer earn tier-1 placements. Protocol stories do. For the full playbook see NFT project PR in 2026.
How much does NFT PR cost?
A fractional senior operator runs NFT PR for $5K–$12K per month, which covers media strategy, tier-1 pitching and KOL coordination. A full launch sprint for a mainnet or major drop runs $15K–$40K over four to eight weeks. Traditional PR agencies typically charge $15K–$45K per month because the retainer funds a multi-person account team that often lacks direct NFT or gaming desk relationships.
Which outlets cover NFT and digital-IP projects?
Tier-1 Web3: CoinDesk, Cointelegraph, Decrypt, The Block, Blockworks. Gaming and culture where the angle warrants it: Polygon (tech), Kotaku, The Verge, Yahoo Finance. For music and creator IP: Rolling Stone, Billboard's Web3 desk, Hypebeast. Mainstream: Forbes, Bloomberg, Benzinga. Coverage depends on a real utility or IP story, not a floor-price update or a mint date.
What does the process and timeline look like?
Week one is a teardown of your current press footprint and the one story worth telling. Week two is narrative, editor mapping and embargo strategy. Weeks three to four are embargo outreach, KOL coordination and the launch push. Sustained retainer work follows monthly. A single launch sprint runs four to eight weeks; the highest-leverage start is six to twelve weeks before your public moment. The Web3 PR campaigns page has the full scope.
