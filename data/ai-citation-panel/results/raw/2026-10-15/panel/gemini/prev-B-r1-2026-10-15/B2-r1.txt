Getting featured in CoinDesk, Cointelegraph, and Decrypt requires tailoring your approach to each outlet's distinct editorial lens. Crypto media is highly specialized, and pitching all three with a single generic press release will almost guarantee silence.

1. Match Your Story to the Outlet's DNA
Outlet | Primary Focus & Audience | Ideal Pitch Angles | Best Approach
CoinDesk | Institutional finance, regulation, macro market trends, protocol-level tech, and canonical industry narratives. The Bloomberg/WSJ of crypto. | Funding rounds ($5M+ led by Tier-1 VCs); Institutional adoption / TradFi integration; Regulatory shifts & legal precedents; Exclusive data or market research reports | Earned Editorial: Must offer a strict Exclusive to a specific beat reporter. Does not run routine product announcements.
Cointelegraph | Broad retail audience, market analysis, Web3 ecosystem news, opinion pieces, and global policy. | Major ecosystem milestones & protocol upgrades; Expert commentary on breaking market news; Thought leadership / Op-Eds; High-impact consumer applications | Hybrid: Pitch beat reporters for earned news, submit Op-Eds for thought leadership, or use paid press distribution wires for guaranteed press section placement.
Decrypt | The intersection of Web3, AI, gaming, pop culture, DePIN, and consumer tech. | AI x Crypto convergence stories; Web3 gaming / entertainment launches; Culture, SocialFi, and consumer adoption; Narrative-driven founder stories | Earned Editorial: Focus on narrative, culture, or human consumer experience rather than dry technical specs. Pitch culture or tech-adjacent reporters.

2. The Pitch Playbook
Step 1: Secure On-Chain & Hard Data Validation
Journalists are inundated with AI-generated pitches and exaggerated protocol stats. Every pitch must be backed by verifiable data:
Funding: Verified press releases or confirmations from lead VCs (e.g., Paradigm, a16z, Dragonfly).
Traction: Publicly auditable on-chain data (Dune Analytics dashboards, DefiLlama tracking, TVL, monthly active wallets).

Step 2: Use the "Exclusive First" Strategy
Never send a pitch blast to all three outlets simultaneously.
Pick the single outlet that best fits the story (e.g., CoinDesk for institutional fundraises; Decrypt for an AI-gaming crossover).
Pitch a beat reporter 3 to 5 days under embargo with explicit terms: "Offering an exclusive on this story until [Date/Time UTC]".
If they pass or don't respond within 24-48 hours, lift the exclusive and offer it to the next outlet.

Step 3: Target Beat Reporters Directly
Generic inbox emails (info@, tips@) rarely yield results.
Find Beat Reporters: Search recent articles on CoinDesk, Cointelegraph, and Decrypt for journalists covering your exact niche (e.g., DeFi, AI agents, policy).
Engage on X (Twitter): Follow them, engage with their recent coverage, and pitch concise context via email or Twitter DMs (if open).
Keep Emails Under 150 Words:
Subject Line: [EXCLUSIVE EMBARGO] [Company Name] raises $X M for [Specific Utility]
Body: 2-3 bullet points outlining Why this matters now and What the verifiable news is, plus a link to a private media kit (Notion page or Google Drive with logos, executive bios, and quote sheets).

Step 4: Pitch News Commentary & Op-Eds
If you don't have breaking news, land coverage through commentary:
Cointelegraph Op-Eds: Submit opinion pieces analyzing broader industry shifts (e.g., post-quantum encryption in crypto, regulatory shifts, MEV developments).
Rapid Reaction Pitching: When major market news breaks (e.g., regulatory rulings, major exploits, macroeconomic shifts), immediately email beat reporters with concise, punchy quotes from your founder or analysts offering expert context.

Key Pitfalls to Avoid
Pitching routine product updates: Standard feature drops or minor mainnet upgrades rarely warrant dedicated articles. Frame them around a macro trend or user adoption milestone instead.
Confusing paid press releases with earned editorial: Paid release syndication services publish to press release subdomains, but they lack the credibility, organic homepage placement, and newsletter distribution of earned editorial coverage.
Ignoring embargo ethics: Breaking an embargo or offering "co-exclusives" to competing outlets will burn reporter relationships permanently.
