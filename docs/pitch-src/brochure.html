03 · Why earned media matters now

In the age of AI, attention is cheap. Credibility isn't.

Buyers, partners and investors no longer just Google you. They ask ChatGPT and Perplexity about you, and those models answer from earned media, not from your homepage.

1. Everyone claims “AI-powered”

Demos are cheap and launches are weekly. Earned coverage is proof that someone with no stake in your business vouched for you. A journalist who chose your story is a third-party endorsement; an ad or a sponsored post is you talking about yourself.

2. AI answers are the new front page

ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, Claude and Google AI Overviews learn who you are from high-authority coverage. Without earned media, the model either ignores you or makes it up. An Ahrefs study of roughly 75,000 brands found branded media mentions correlate with visibility in AI answers at 0.66, against 0.33 for Domain Rating and 0.22 for backlinks. Being talked about beats backlinks three to one. PR is now an AI-distribution channel.

3. Investors diligence in public

Before any meeting, investors search you on Google, ChatGPT and X. What's written about you and your founders is the first data room, long before the real one opens. If a raise is anywhere on your roadmap, founder profiling is the highest-leverage PR you can do.

Organic first, paid only when it helps

Organic coverage is hard: an editor has to choose your story. That's exactly why it carries credibility. Paid coverage is easy: you pay, it publishes within a week, it's labelled “sponsored”, and readers, journalists and AI models discount it accordingly. I work organic-first, and build paid media strategies only when you need to fast-track coverage, quoted transparently per outlet.

Most newly funded Web3 and AI startups go from zero AI citations to 8–15 inside one quarter when the schema, content and PR layers run together.

10 · Answers

Questions founders ask before booking.

What does a fractional PR manager do for a Web3 or AI startup?

Runs the full PR function on a part-time retainer instead of an agency contract: narrative, embargoed press, founder profiling, KOL waves, APAC localisation and launch comms. Founder access is direct, and accountability is tied to placements in named outlets rather than billable hours.

Which Tier-1 publications have you placed clients in?

Forbes, CoinDesk, Cointelegraph, Decrypt, The Block, Blockworks, Bitcoin Magazine, Benzinga, Yahoo Finance, AI Magazine, The Defiant, CryptoSlate, CryptoNews, CryptoPotato and CryptoDaily globally. Regionally: BloomingBit, TokenPost and Coinness in Korea; CryptoTimes JP and Coinpost in Japan; ChainCatcher and Jinse for Greater China; Inc42, e27 and Economic Times for India and SEA.

How do I get featured in CoinDesk or Cointelegraph?

Lead with a category-level story, not a funding or launch announcement. Map the right reporter on the desk that covers your space, write an under-180-word pitch with a dated news peg, and offer a real embargo confirmed in writing 7 to 10 days out.

Do you handle AI startups outside of crypto?

Yes. The same playbook — category narrative, founder profiling, journalist mapping, podcast tours, op-ed ghostwriting — applies whether or not the company is on-chain. AI Magazine, Forbes AI coverage, The Information and category-defining op-eds are the typical surface area.

How do you build AI-search visibility for a startup with no Google presence?

Start with the entity, not the keyword: schema-marked founder and organisation profiles, named-source PR placements that ChatGPT, Perplexity, Claude and Gemini already cite, FAQ content that answers the exact buyer question, and a 90-day citation panel to measure which engines pick the brand up.

Which regions do you cover?

Korea, Japan, Vietnam, China, Singapore, India and the UAE, plus global English-language press. Each market gets native-language placements, regional KOL waves and on-ground access at Korea Blockchain Week, Token2049 Singapore and Dubai, Japan Blockchain Week and ETHIndia.

How do we start?

Book a 30-minute teardown. Bring your roadmap, your funding stage and the one publication you want to be in. You leave with a scoped plan and an honest read on whether fractional PR is the right call — or whether an agency or in-house hire is a better fit. No agency-style sales process.
