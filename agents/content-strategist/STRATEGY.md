# Content Strategy — 4Ps positioning

## Problem (who has a problem you can solve, and what is it)

Founders running Web3 or AI startups, typically pre-Series-A through Series B, who need PR coverage but cannot justify or wait for a traditional agency.

The shape of the problem they show up with:

- A funding round closing in 4 to 8 weeks. No PR plan.
- A token launch in 60 to 90 days. No journalist relationships.
- A category position that nobody understands, and competitors who are getting better press for worse products.
- An APAC expansion where the local press cycle is a mystery.
- A crisis (rug pull allegation, exchange listing fallout, founder controversy) that hit yesterday.

The pain shape is "I know I need PR but the agencies want $20K/month and a 6-month contract and assign a junior account team."

## Promise (what you actually deliver)

- A senior operator with a direct line into the Tier-1 trade press, embedded with the founder.
- Outcomes tied to placements in named outlets, not to billable hours.
- A fractional retainer that costs a fraction of an agency contract because there are no account-team layers.
- A 30-minute scoped teardown call that produces a real plan, not a sales pitch.

## Process (the operating model)

The work has six surfaces:

1. **Web3 PR Campaigns** — end-to-end strategic narrative + tier-1 outreach.
2. **Founder Profiling & Op-Eds** — op-ed ghostwriting + podcast tour + speaker placement.
3. **KOL & Influencer Marketing** — three-wave launch sequencing across 200+ vetted creators.
4. **APAC Localisation & Access** — Korean, Japanese, Chinese diaspora, Indian, MENA press + on-ground events.
5. **Token Launch PR / TGE Communications** — pre-TGE positioning, embargo coordination, exchange listing comms.
6. **Cybersecurity PR** — analyst relations (Gartner, Forrester, IDC, KuppingerCole), security trade press, breach comms.
7. **AI Startup PR** — Forbes, AI Magazine, Decrypt's AI desk, TechCrunch AI, The Information.
8. **Content Writing** — op-eds, whitepapers, founder essays.

Each surface has its own service page on the site, its own pricing, and its own playbook. Cross-link liberally between blog posts and service pages.

## Person (who Shilika is to the reader)

The senior operator who has run the campaigns the reader is trying to figure out. Six years, 50+ protocols, APAC operator. Currently at Myosin. Quotable presence in Forbes / CoinDesk / Cointelegraph / Decrypt / Blockworks / AI Magazine.

Tone: smart colleague debriefing you over coffee, not consultant pitching you in a deck.

## Channel data + signup attribution

Inbound flows (mapped to landing pages):

- Direct (Tier-1 placement → Google "shilika jain" → site → Calendly)
- Organic search (long-tail Web3/AI PR queries → /blog or /services/* → Calendly)
- LinkedIn (founder profiling → Shilika's profile → site → Calendly)
- Word of mouth (existing client referral → /work case study → Calendly)
- AI assistant citations (Perplexity, Claude, ChatGPT cite a blog post → site → Calendly)

The blog targets the organic search and AI assistant citation flows. Every post should be:

- Optimized for a specific long-tail query the buyer is likely typing
- Set up to be cited cleanly by AI assistants (clean structure, short citable paragraphs, declarative claims)
- Linked to the relevant service page so the Calendly conversion path is one click away

## Conversion path per post

1. Reader lands on `/blog/<slug>` from search or AI citation
2. Reader scans the title and first 150 words. Decides whether to stay.
3. Reader reads the post.
4. Reader hits the "Book a 30-min teardown with Shilika" CTA at the bottom OR clicks a related service page.
5. Booking on Calendly = top-of-funnel conversion event.

The CTA needs to make sense after the post they just read. Default text is fine. If the post is about a specific service, use a service-specific CTA label.
