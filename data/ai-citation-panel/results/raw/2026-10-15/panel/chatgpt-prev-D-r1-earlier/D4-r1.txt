What does cybersecurity PR cost for a Series B vendor in 2026?

For a Series B cybersecurity company, a reasonable 2026 planning budget for public relations is $8,000-$20,000 per month for a specialist PR agency, with premium enterprise-focused firms potentially costing more.

The right budget depends on whether you want steady media coverage, executive thought leadership, major product announcements, or category leadership with enterprise buyers and investors.

For a Series B vendor, I'd evaluate PR as a growth investment tied to credibility, pipeline influence, and market positioning - not simply the number of press mentions.

What should a $15K-$20K retainer buy?

At this spend, I'd expect a defined scope covering:
- Media relations: Targeted pitches to cybersecurity and enterprise technology journalists, not just press-release syndication.
- Executive visibility: Founder/CEO positioning, interview preparation, contributed articles and commentary.
- Security-specific storytelling: Threat research, vulnerability findings, incident trends, customer outcomes or original data, where available.
- Analyst relations: Planned briefings and feedback loops with relevant firms such as Gartner, Forrester or IDC, where in scope. Formal analyst evaluations are not guaranteed.
- Measurement: Relevant placements, message pull-through, share of voice versus named competitors, referral traffic and evidence of influence on enterprise opportunities.
- Crisis readiness: Agreed escalation procedures and support boundaries for security incidents or vulnerability disclosures.

Treat these as proposed deliverables to negotiate into the contract, rather than guaranteed outputs. Published agency benchmarks suggest this middle tier is where ongoing media, executive, analyst and AI-search visibility work can be combined.

How I'd decide what to spend
- If the immediate goal is a funding announcement or product launch, consider a fixed-scope project rather than committing to a large retainer.
- If the goal is enterprise credibility and pipeline over the next 6-12 months, use a specialist agency with demonstrable cybersecurity media relationships.
- If you already have an in-house communications lead, a fractional specialist may provide better value than paying for a full agency team.
- If you need a global program or substantial crisis coverage, budget for the higher tier and define exactly what on-call support includes.

One caution: many public pricing guides are published by agencies selling these services, so treat their figures as directional benchmarks rather than neutral market averages.
