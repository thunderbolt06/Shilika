Getting covered in these outlets mostly comes down to having real news, fitting each outlet's focus, and pitching like a journalist would want it.

1. Have an actual story. Funding with named investors, product launches with real usage, exclusive data, or a strong opinion on regulation. A pitch claiming a $20M raise without naming investors gets dismissed, while naming firms like Paradigm or Pantera gets better treatment because it offers independent verification. Expect fact checks too: reporters will check on-chain data for user claims and DefiLlama for TVL claims. [instantpress] [instantpress]

2. Match the outlet.

CoinDesk: favors regulatory and institutional angles and skips fluff. It works on embargo, rewards exclusives, and wants a clear hook, verifiable claims, and a named executive. [mexc] [everything-pr]

Decrypt: wants accessible stories tying crypto to culture, business, or tech trends. Good for product launches. [mexc]

Cointelegraph: open to varied stories but expects a clear news peg up front. [mexc]

3. Pitch the right reporter. Find who covers your beat (DeFi, gaming, policy) and email them directly. Tip lines also exist: tips@coindesk.com and tips@decrypt.co. [instantpress]

4. Keep the pitch tight. Short, direct, credibility first, and under 400 words. Avoid price talk and token shilling. Offer exclusive data when you can. [instantpress]

5. Other routes. Write op eds as a founder, speak at big events like Consensus or TOKEN2049, or become a go-to expert source reporters quote. Paid sponsored posts and press release distribution exist too, but they're labeled and carry less credibility than earned coverage.

6. Follow up once, then build relationships over time. Being a helpful source matters more than any single pitch.
