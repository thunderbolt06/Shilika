FAQ

Airdrop marketing questions, answered.

What does an airdrop marketing agency actually do?
Beyond coordinating KOL posts, a specialist airdrop agency builds the narrative layer that prevents the farming headline. That means framing eligibility criteria as product adoption data, preparing journalist briefs that give editors a real angle, running a KOL wave timed to the snapshot, and managing the press embargo through the TGE window. Shilika Jain runs this full comms motion as a fractional senior operator for $5K–$12K/month. See the token launch PR service for the full scope.
How much does airdrop marketing cost?
A full-service agency running an airdrop campaign typically charges $15K–$45K/month because the retainer covers a multi-person account team. A fractional senior operator delivers the same senior scope for $5K–$12K/month. A single launch sprint, covering the pre-snapshot window through TGE, runs $15K–$40K over four to eight weeks. KOL costs sit on top: nano creators ($200–$1.5K each), micro ($500–$5K), mid-tier ($10K–$30K) and macro ($25K–$100K+).
Which outlets can you place an airdrop story in?
Tier-1 crypto with direct editor relationships: CoinDesk, Cointelegraph, The Block, Decrypt, Blockworks, The Defiant. Mainstream where the angle warrants it: Forbes, Bloomberg, Yahoo Finance, Benzinga. Regional distribution for APAC participation: BloomingBit and TokenPost (Korea), CryptoTimes JP and CoinPost (Japan), ChainCatcher and Jinse (China), Inc42 (India), e27 (SEA). Outlet selection depends on the news hook, not a blanket wire blast.
What is the right timeline to start airdrop PR?
Six to twelve weeks before the snapshot date is the minimum lead time for useful narrative work. That window allows positioning to be built, journalist relationships to be warmed, and KOL briefs to be drafted before the distribution mechanics are public. Starting after the snapshot is announced leaves almost no room to change how the story reads. For the full pre-TGE timeline see the airdrop PR strategy playbook.
