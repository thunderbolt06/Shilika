How to get featured in CoinDesk, Cointelegraph and Decrypt in 2026

If you're a crypto founder, Web3 startup, blockchain protocol, exchange, AI/Web3 company or PR agency, the key is to approach these publications as three independent newsrooms - not three websites where you simply submit a press release.

There are three main routes to visibility:
Earned editorial coverage: A journalist independently decides your story is newsworthy. You don't pay for the coverage, and publication isn't guaranteed.
Guest articles and expert commentary: You contribute original analysis, opinions or industry expertise, subject to editorial approval.
Paid media and sponsored content: You purchase an explicitly commercial placement, subject to the publication's policies.

The distinction matters. CoinDesk explicitly states that its editorial team does not sell articles or coverage. Cointelegraph also maintains a formal separation between editorial and commercial operations.

2. What actually makes a crypto story publishable in 2026?
The strongest pitch gives an editor a reason to cover the story now, not merely a reason your company wants publicity.
Original data or research: Examples: a new analysis of stablecoin usage, DeFi risk, on-chain activity, wallet behaviour or institutional adoption. Include methodology and verifiable evidence.
A genuine news event: A material funding round, acquisition, major integration, regulatory development, security incident or significant product launch. Explain the significance beyond the announcement.
A credible expert perspective: An informed response to a current market development, with a distinct thesis, evidence and an expert available for interview.
A wider industry implication: Why an event matters to users, investors, developers, institutions or regulators - not just to your token holders or customers.
These are practical pitching recommendations, not guarantees of publication. Cointelegraph's editorial policy says coverage is determined by news value and journalistic merit; Decrypt specifically asks for clear arguments backed by facts and original sources.

3. How to pitch: a practical workflow
Choose one story, not one publicity objective. Write down the actual development, why it matters now, and what makes it independently interesting.
Research the right journalist. Find recent stories about the same subject, identify the reporter or editor who covers it, and refer to a specific relevant article.
Prepare an evidence pack. Include a concise announcement or research brief, original data, source documents, relevant links, spokesperson details, and any material disclosures.
Send a short, personalised email. Lead with the news peg and its significance. Offer an interview, exclusive data or access to credible sources where appropriate.
Follow up selectively. If there is no reply, send one concise follow-up after a few business days. For breaking news, contact the newsroom promptly and make the timing explicit.
Keep editorial and paid campaigns separate. If you need a guaranteed commercial placement, ask the sales or partnerships team about available products instead of asking a journalist to sell editorial coverage.

6. Common mistakes to avoid
Sending the same generic press release to every journalist.
Leading with claims like "revolutionary," "world-leading" or "the next 100x token."
Offering unsupported user, transaction, revenue or market-share figures.
Writing a guest article that is essentially a sales pitch.
Asking journalists to guarantee coverage in exchange for money.
Treating sponsored content as independent editorial endorsement.
Paying intermediaries who promise guaranteed organic coverage without verifiable terms and clear disclosure.
