The big change in 2026 is that the old "pay for posts" model is weaker. X banned InfoFi apps like Kaito and Cookie DAO from its data pipelines, and Kaito shut down Yaps and moved to a stricter, tiered creator setup closer to traditional marketing. So today it's about fewer, better creators and real tracking. [blockeden] [mexc]

Here's a simple playbook:

1. Set one clear goal. Pick a single target like testnet users, TVL, wallet signups, or Discord members. "Awareness" alone is hard to measure and easy to waste money on.
2. Pick creators by audience, not follower count. Check engagement quality, reply authenticity, and past campaign results. Many large accounts have a high share of bots or inactive followers, so mid-sized niche creators (DeFi, L2, gaming, etc.) often perform better.
3. Mix tiers. Use 1 or 2 big names for credibility, plus 10 to 30 smaller, trusted voices for depth and repetition.
4. Give them substance, not scripts. Offer early access, a call with the founders, or a real feature to try. Threads, walkthroughs, and honest reviews beat copy-pasted shill posts.
5. Track everything. Give each KOL unique referral links or codes, and measure onchain actions from those wallets, not just likes and views.
6. Pay for results where possible. Combine a base fee with bonuses tied to real users or retention. Avoid paying mainly in your own token with no vesting, since it often gets dumped.
7. Go beyond X. Spread across YouTube, Telegram, Farcaster, podcasts, and regional communities (Korea, Vietnam, Turkey, etc.) so one platform's policy change can't kill your campaign.
8. Stay compliant. Make sure every paid post is clearly disclosed. Rules on crypto promotions are tightening in many places (EU MiCA, UK, US), so check with a lawyer for your target regions.
9. Review and cut. After 2 to 4 weeks, drop the creators who didn't move your metric and double down on the ones who did.
