# Tangem Marketing Teardown: Sell Ownership, Not Hardware

In June 2026 I audited Tangem's marketing for my teardown series. The product is strong and the opportunity is big. My main note: Tangem's biggest competitor is not another hardware wallet, it is every centralized platform that convinces users they do not need self-custody. So I would stop selling a device and start selling ownership.

## Why did I start a marketing teardown series?

I wanted to show, in public, what Web3 brands could do better on visibility by mapping their current strategy on social platforms. Developers have built in public for years. Marketers rarely do.

> It's always about developers buidl'ing in public, now it's about marketers building in public!!
>
> [Shilika Jain on X, 1 Jun 2026](https://x.com/Shilika_jain/status/2061449096146325839)

Tangem was first. A teardown is not a takedown. Everything below is what I would do if I ran marketing there next quarter, and it only works because the product already holds up.

## Who is Tangem really competing with?

The obvious answer is other hardware wallets. I think that framing is too small. The person choosing between two wallets has already decided to hold their own keys. The much larger group is everyone who leaves assets on a centralized platform because it feels easier and safe enough.

That changes the job of marketing. A feature comparison wins the first group. Only a bigger idea moves the second.

> If I were to run marketing next quarter, I'd stop selling hardware wallets and start selling ownership.
>
> [Shilika Jain on X, 4 Jun 2026](https://x.com/Shilika_jain/status/2062437697978008001)

Selling ownership means the story is about the user's money and who controls it. The wallet becomes the proof, not the pitch. This is the same category-level thinking I describe in my [narrative and positioning playbook](/playbook/web3-narrative-positioning-strategy-2026).

## Which campaign ideas would I run, and why would they earn coverage?

Journalists do not cover product pages. They cover data, tension and trends. Each idea in my post was built to give a reporter one of those.

| Idea | What it is | Why it would earn coverage |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Not Your Keys Index | A live tracker of exchange failures, frozen funds, hacks and regulatory actions | It turns recurring industry news into a standing reference. Every new incident becomes a reason to cite the index |
| Trapped yield report | A quarterly report with Aave, Morpho, Ethena and Pendle on how much yield sits idle on centralized exchanges | It is a headline, not a product announcement. Newsworthy, non-commercial and impact driven |
| Self-Custody Adoption Report | The industry's first, published with Nansen, Arkham, Delphi Digital or Glassnode | Original data with a credible research partner is the easiest thing to pitch and the most likely to be cited again |
| Blind setup challenge | 100 real people in a room setting up Ledger, Trezor and Tangem, with results published | A real-world test gives reporters a result and human reactions, not a claim |
| Self-Custodied badge | A universal badge that wallets, protocols and apps can integrate | It makes self-custody visible across the ecosystem and gives partners a reason to talk about it |

The data ideas matter most to me.

> If you own the data, you ace the narrative. That's what really matters.
>
> [Shilika Jain on X, 4 Jun 2026](https://x.com/Shilika_jain/status/2062437697978008001)

An index also solves the timing problem in [newsjacking](/playbook/crypto-newsjacking-2026). When a story breaks you are not scrambling for an angle, because the tracker already exists and the reporter already knows where to look.

## How would I turn self-custody into a movement?

Reports reach journalists and analysts. Users need something they can join. Two ideas from the post do that work:

- **The Great Exchange Exit.** Incentivize users to move assets into self-custody and share their stories. User stories are what make a campaign travel on social, and they give local media a human angle.
- **"Your stablecoins deserve self-custody too."** A partner campaign with USDC or Ethena around one simple idea. Stablecoin holders are a wide audience, and a single line is easy to repeat.

> Self-custody must turn into a social movement to capture eyeballs!!
>
> [Shilika Jain on X, 4 Jun 2026](https://x.com/Shilika_jain/status/2062437697978008001)

## Which narratives and opinions would I push?

I would give the brand a few strong lines and repeat them everywhere, from founder posts to op-eds:

- Exchanges are becoming banks.
- Self-custody is the next fintech.
- Hardware wallets should be as easy as Apple Pay.

Then I would ask the uncomfortable question out loud: why are hardware wallets still harder to use than Apple Pay in 2026? I also suggested a controversial piece titled "Why 90% of hardware wallets are still too difficult to use." That title is a provocation from my post, not a statistic, and it would need the blind setup challenge or similar data behind it before it ran.

A brand that criticizes its own category earns trust, because it reads as honest and not as promotion. It is also the kind of opinion an editor will actually take as a contributed piece.

## What can other founders take from this teardown?

Most of these ideas cost less than a paid campaign. What they need is a point of view and the discipline to keep publishing. That is the difference I keep coming back to when founders ask me about [earned versus paid media](/playbook/earned-vs-paid-media-web3-2026).

> Most crypto companies think PR = announcements. The best brands create narratives so powerful that every announcement becomes news.
>
> [Shilika Jain on X, 4 Jun 2026](https://x.com/Shilika_jain/status/2062437697978008001)

Three questions to ask about your own brand:

1. Who is your real competitor, the similar product or the default behavior?
2. What data could you own that the media would cite every quarter?
3. What is the one sentence you want people to associate with you?

For Tangem, my answer to the third is in the post: it should aim to become the brand people think of when they hear "Take control of your money." That is the brief I would hand to any [Web3 PR campaign](/services/web3-pr-campaigns) team working on it.
