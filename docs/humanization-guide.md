# Humanization Guide

The rules the validator enforces. Anything in **Hard Rules** is a regex match — if it fires, the draft is blocked. **Soft Rules** are judgment-based; aim to honor them but the validator does not enforce them.

## Hard Rules

- No em-dashes (—). Use periods, commas, or restructure.
- No ellipsis (…) for dramatic effect.
- No arrow symbols (→, ⇒, ←).
- No openers: "Here's why," "Here's how," "Here's the thing," "Let's dive in," "Let's break it down," "Let's unpack."
- No "the reality is," "the truth is." Just state the thing.
- No "In today's [world / landscape / environment / economy]."
- No corporate AI fluff: "game-changer," "robust," "cutting-edge," "seamless," "navigate" (when not literal).
- No summary openers: "In conclusion," "To sum up," "The bottom line is."
- No horizontal-rule dividers in prose. Use headers.
- No "delve" / "delves" / "delving."
- No "tapestry" or "tapestries" as a metaphor.
- No "in essence."

## Soft Rules (writer judgment, not validator-enforced)

- Use contractions ("don't," "can't," "you're").
- Vary paragraph length. AI defaults to uniform three-sentence paragraphs.
- Don't end with a summary. End with a sharp point or just stop.
- Maximum one or two rhetorical questions per post.
- First person beats third person.
- Specific outlets and people beat "many outlets" and "industry leaders."
- Concrete numbers beat vague claims ("30%" not "many").
- Reference real tools, companies, and outlets. Vague generalities sound like AI.

## Why these rules exist

Em-dashes are the single most reliable AI tell. The opener patterns ("Here's why," "Let's dive in") show up in roughly 1 in 3 AI-generated paragraphs and almost never in human-written prose at a sentence start. The summary closer is the second strongest tell.

Google reads engagement signals to rank pages. Readers see AI tells and tune out. Tuned-out readers bounce. High bounce + low time-on-page = the post stops ranking. The validator's hard rules are the bouncers at the door.
