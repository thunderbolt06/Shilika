FAQ

Korea crypto PR questions, answered.

What is Korea crypto PR and who is it for?
Korea crypto PR means securing coverage in Korean-language outlets such as BloomingBit and TokenPost, activating vetted Korean KOLs and KakaoTalk community leads, and building the Korean-language narrative that makes a project credible to Korean retail traders, institutions and exchange listing committees. It is for projects planning a Korean exchange listing, attending Korea Blockchain Week, or building a retail community in Seoul. For the full scope of APAC localisation see the APAC PR service page.
How much does Korea crypto PR cost?
A fractional senior operator running Korea and Japan together typically costs $5K–$12K/month. A full agency with in-country staff will run $15K–$45K/month. KOL activations in Korea are priced separately: nano-tier Korean KOLs run $200–$1.5K per post, micro-tier $500–$5K, and mid-tier $10K–$30K. A Korea-specific launch sprint covering KBW plus two months of follow-on coverage runs $15K–$40K in total.
Which Korean outlets and KOLs can you place my project in?
Primary Korean crypto outlets: BloomingBit and TokenPost for editorial coverage, plus Hankyung and JoongAng for mainstream crossover when the institutional angle is strong. Korean KOL channels are matched by audience vertical: DeFi, Layer 1, gaming or RWA, with engagement and follower quality checked before any activation. Japan is available in parallel via CryptoTimes JP and CoinPost.
How does the Korea PR process work and how long does it take?
The first two weeks cover the Korean narrative audit and media map. Week three onwards is active outreach to Korean editors and a briefing to the KOL shortlist. First BloomingBit or TokenPost coverage typically lands within four to six weeks if there is a real news hook. KBW timing compresses the window: embargo prep for KBW should start at least eight weeks before the event.
